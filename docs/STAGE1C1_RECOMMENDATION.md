# Stage 1C.1 decision and proposed next phase

**Retain the reconciliation and controlled diagnostics as research infrastructure. Archive the fitted context arms; do not activate them or the diagnostic probability wrapper.** The primary passing-yards endpoint fails its preregistered useful-gain and uncertainty gates. The sealed final period must not be opened to rescue that result.

## What explains the earlier C1/C2 result?

1. **Backbone and context are different effects.** On 2,288 development passing games, frozen Stage 1B.1 point MAE is 68.1402, the Stage 1C player-only backbone is 68.4551, and its combined-context version is 68.2255. Returning to the native Stage 1B.1 recipe with context produces 68.0901. The controlled context gain is only 0.0500 yards (0.073%); paired 95% block intervals are [−0.3990, +0.5367] at four weeks and [−0.3442, +0.5458] at eight weeks. Backbone deterioration and a small context improvement partly offset each other. The factorial interaction is uncertain; this does not prove a universally better backbone.
2. **Missing positional data was real, but repairing it is not sufficient.** TE receiving complete groups increase from 1,439 to 4,008; WR from 2,008 to 4,010; RB receiving from 2,756 to 4,012 of 4,016. Reconciliation reduces rushing-yard MAE by 0.4362 relative to the otherwise identical legacy-source context arm, yet the reconciled combined arm still scores 22.5847 versus the native player-only 22.5632. Passing groups were already complete and their predictions do not change between source arms. Seventeen team-game stat-credit discrepancies remain unresolved.
3. **Context sensitivity is weak or unstable, not evidence for a new weight.** In prior-year diagnostic windows, combined-model passing C1 block permutation increases MAE by an average 0.4268 yards, while C2 permutation changes it by −0.0256, with substantial fold/repetition variation. Some admitted point methods ignore context entirely. Redundancy, limited independent team-game ESS and revised-source publication uncertainty constrain interpretation. These diagnostics did not change fitting or selection.
4. **Team volume does not determine player opportunity.** In the hindsight-only five-game ratio strawman, receiving target-count MAE is 1.8884; replacing only team targets with actual totals reduces it to 1.7272, whereas substituting actual player share reduces it to 0.7493. For RB carries the analogous figures are 3.8639, 3.1932 and 2.0781. These are oracle substitutions, not attainable forecast gains. In Shapley accounting, share represents about 56% of the strawman's reception error, 64% of RB carry error and 76% of QB carry error. Actual opportunity variation can include unpredictable in-game exits and role changes.
5. **Passing remains a mixed volume/share/efficiency problem.** Passing-yard strawman error accounting assigns 21.5704 yards to team volume, 20.5372 to player share and 26.3329 to efficiency. Actual team attempts alone change strawman MAE from 68.4405 to 59.5721, not to a perfect forecast. This decomposition is not of the fitted Ridge/boosting model and cannot establish which factor is learnable at T−60.

## Cohorts and probability limitations

The combined native-backbone model is slightly worse on point MAE in eight of nine pooled development markets; its passing gain is negligible. Passing results are unchanged in 2022/2023, worse in 2024, better in 2025 and worse in the already-viewed 2026 monitoring period (68.0315 versus 67.1188). These reversals prevent a robust improvement claim.

The prespecified RB receiving cohort improves by 0.1172 yards across 4,685 games, with four/eight-week intervals both crossing zero. Its MAE worsens in 2022 and improves in 2023–25; interval-score change is +2.8713 (worse), despite small proper-score gains. There was no subgroup-specific fitting, retrospective segment selection or selective deployment.

All new arms deliberately share one diagnostic probability method to remove methodology confounding. It is not a replacement selected for calibration quality. With identical native player-only point predictions, passing-yard log loss changes from frozen Stage 1B.1's 0.4411 to 0.4585, and nominal 90% coverage falls from 90.65% to 87.85%. Combined-context coverage is 87.54%. These regressions support retaining the frozen probability benchmark. Full reliability bands, intervals, all nine markets, seasons and descriptive cohorts are supplied, including unfavourable results.

All evidence uses previously inspected development/monitoring data and retrospective revised sources. No new independent performance claim, genuine bookmaker quote, profitable betting backtest or live-valid pre-decision data certification is made.

## Is C3 justified?

**A narrow player-opportunity allocation feasibility study is justified conditionally; broad C3 implementation or activation is not yet justified.** Player-share headroom is clearer for receptions and rushing counts than for passing-yard accuracy. That prioritisation is a hypothesis for a new preregistered study, not a selected betting segment.

If approved separately, prioritise:

1. **Opportunity-history data gate.** Test source-backed zero-opportunity histories separately from context/backbone changes. The present audit identifies 106 QB, 475 RB, 3,028 TE and 2,092 WR active missing-box records; most relevant count ledgers support true zero opportunities, but they have not been inserted into frozen evaluation labels or candidate histories. Keep unresolved fields unknown, resolve the 17 residual team-game credit discrepancies when defensible, and version recoveries. Collect actual receipt/publication timestamps prospectively for roster/usage inputs. Historical proxy availability must remain labelled retrospective.
2. **Count-first allocation experiment.** Before adding injury or personnel logic, preregister player-only, team-count-only, player-share-only and combined feasible models on identical eligible rows. Target receptions/carry opportunity mechanisms as scientific questions while still reporting all nine markets and passing-yard safeguards. Require coherent teammate totals and explicit inactive/missing/zero distinctions; the oracle strawman does not provide these constraints. Select solely in prior windows and preserve untouched benchmarks/final period.
3. **Passing-specific feasibility.** Validate the plays/dropbacks→attempts bridge and separate starter participation/share uncertainty from conditional passing efficiency. Do not presume that copying an improved team-volume prediction into each player solves the point-model problem. The present passing and completions regressions are blockers to a blanket context promotion.
4. **Probability and interval control.** Hold the frozen validated research distribution methodology constant initially, rather than adopting this study's simpler wrapper. Any subsequent distribution change requires its own chronological selection, proper-score/reliability and interval-sharpness evaluation. Require practically useful, stable benefits without material cohort, bias or calibration harm; document multiplicity and inspected-data limits.

No C3–C6 code, production change, provider configuration, dashboard hosting, merge or deployment has been performed by this recommendation. Approval of the next protocol is required before implementation.
